Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript, “Brief Retrieval Practice and Delayed Recall in a Classroom Simulation: A Randomized Pilot Study,” for consideration as Original Research in the Journal of Classroom Learning Methods.

Our study examines whether a brief retrieval practice activity affects recall of learning material one week later. In this single-center randomized pilot study, 96 university students were assigned to retrieval practice or repeated reading, with 48 participants in each group. All participants completed the day 7 assessment. On a 10-point recall test, mean scores were 7.4 in the retrieval practice group and 6.5 in the repeated reading group. The mean difference was 0.9 points (95% CI, 0.2–1.6; p = 0.012).

These results provide evidence of a difference in delayed recall within this pilot sample. Their interpretation remains limited to the measured outcome and the classroom simulation setting. The study did not assess long-term course performance, clinical outcomes, or sustained effects in actual classrooms. Its contribution is to provide pilot evidence to inform subsequent evaluations of brief retrieval activities in real courses, where their relevance to ongoing learning can be examined.

The manuscript aligns with the journal’s scope through its empirical evaluation of a learning activity and its measurement of delayed recall. Its clearly defined pilot design and implications for subsequent research also match the journal’s stated emphasis on clearly reported pilot studies. We believe it will interest readers investigating classroom learning methods, instructional activities, and learning assessment.

We confirm that this research is the authors’ original work. The full manuscript has not been published elsewhere or posted as a preprint and is not currently under consideration by another journal. All authors have reviewed the manuscript and approved its submission to this journal. All authors confirm that they have no competing interests to disclose.
