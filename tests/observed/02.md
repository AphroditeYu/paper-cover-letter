Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Screen Breaks and Self-Reported Concentration in University Students,” as an Original Research article in the Journal of Everyday Learning Research.

The study examines whether the frequency of screen breaks is associated with self-reported concentration in university students. In a cross-sectional questionnaire study of 180 students, screen-break frequency was positively correlated with self-reported concentration (Pearson’s r = 0.28). This observation provides preliminary evidence to inform further research on everyday learning habits.

The study’s contribution is a focused account of an association in a learning context. Its interpretation is necessarily limited by the cross-sectional design, absence of random assignment, and lack of covariate adjustment. The result does not establish temporal order or a causal effect, and potential confounding remains unresolved. It offers a starting point for future longitudinal or experimental research to examine the relationship more closely.

The manuscript fits the journal’s stated focus on behavioral, cognitive, and educational research in learning contexts by connecting a daily behavior with students’ perceptions of their concentration. Presenting this finding alongside its design limitations also aligns with the journal’s emphasis on explaining how methodological constraints affect interpretation.
