Dear Editor,

We submit “Help-Seeking Preferences in First-Year Study Groups” for consideration as a Research Article in the Journal of Student Learning Experiences.

Drawing on interviews with 18 first-year students, the study uses thematic analysis to examine participants’ accounts of seeking help in study groups. Interviewees described a preference for approaching familiar peers and concerns that asking questions might expose a lack of ability. These accounts offer contextual qualitative insights into students’ preferences and concerns when seeking support from peers.

The study’s contribution is to inform the design of future learning-support activities by drawing attention to peer familiarity and concerns about perceived ability. Its findings provide qualitative insights for subsequent design work; the study does not measure behavioral frequencies, academic outcomes, or intervention effects.

The manuscript aligns with the journal’s focus on student learning experiences, peer interaction, and learning support. Its interview-based approach also fits the journal’s interest in contextualized research, making it relevant to readers considering students’ perspectives when developing learning-support activities.

We confirm that the work is original and that the manuscript is not currently under consideration by another journal.
