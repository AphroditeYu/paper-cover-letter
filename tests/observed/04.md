Dear Editor,

Please consider “Peer Feedback Preferences in an Introductory Design Course,” authored by Chen Li, Mira Stone, and Bo Lin, as a Research Article in the Journal of Classroom Design Studies.

The study explores students’ preferences for peer feedback in an introductory design course. Through semi-structured interviews with 12 students and thematic analysis, we identified two themes: students wanted feedback to be specific and actionable, and they valued respectful expression from their peers. These findings offer context-specific insights for designing peer feedback activities within courses. The study did not measure changes in academic performance, and its contribution concerns students’ reported preferences and experiences.

The manuscript aligns with the journal’s focus on course design, classroom interaction, and learner experiences, as well as its inclusion of qualitative research. Its examination of how students prefer to receive peer feedback may inform readers considering the design of classroom feedback activities.

I confirm that the research is the authors’ original work, that the full manuscript has not been formally published elsewhere, and that it is not currently under review at another journal. As corresponding author, I, Chen Li, approve this submission and have no competing interests to declare.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.

Chen Li
